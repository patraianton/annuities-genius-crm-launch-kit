How this was made

Built September 30 to October 1 by research and writing agents I run. Steps one to four below say what was read and what it changed; sources are linked in each step and listed below.

Four review rounds on the night of September 30, each by six simulated reader roles (an annuity agent, an IMO owner, the hiring manager, AG's house voice, a design critic, a fact-checker), all from one model family and all reading the same research folder. A separate audit on October 1 found three existing features that the research had called missing; this version carries its corrections.

After the rounds I wrote down what had slipped through as rules, with a program that checks a draft against them: a number with no source, a promise in AG's name with no owner, a line cut in review that came back, a sentence on this page that is missing from its text file.

On October 2 it gave 189 findings on the October 1 version of this kit; after cuts and rewrites it gives none.

The same day an agent on another model family (OpenAI's) tried to overturn the nine product facts in the research table: six held, two were narrowed to what their source shows, one could not be checked from public pages.

What none of this catches: a false sentence with no number and no flagged word. The copy was written and reviewed by one model family, and no annuity agent has read it.

The rules, the program and the record of this run: github.com/patraianton/ag-growth-rules (this run).

How we'd know it worked

  • The launch number: the share of new cases with a client message sent from AG.
  • We run our own funnel on it first: every Wednesday attendee becomes a client in our AG account and gets the same email-and-call follow-up from it. If webinar-to-trial doesn't move, the promise isn't ready.
  • IMO side: share of Case Design requests that arrive with a conversation attached.
  • Money: seat retention of agents who message clients from AG vs. agents on the same plan and tenure who don't.

Four questions before this ships

  1. I've assumed retention: keep solo and IMO-sourced agents from paying for a second tool. If the CRM's real job is moving IMO desks onto more seats, the plan flips: the IMO note becomes the lead piece, and calling and texting become the Enterprise upgrade rather than part of every plan. Which is it? And every plan, or Prime+ and up? In 2023-2025 in-platform texting sat on the mid and upper plans only, and Client Management left Starter in June 2026.
  2. Which of our IMO partners hand their agents their own CRM, and will they see this as help or as competition? My default: brief every IMO a week ahead and offer the co-signed email. Any IMO that sees competition gets a founder call first, and its agents wait until it agrees.
  3. How many of our agents pay for GoHighLevel under any name? Cancel reasons and one poll question at the webinar would tell us. If few, this launch carries the year; if many, a switch campaign starts the week after launch, with a CSV import (AG has none today) and a switch offer.
  4. GoHighLevel's third-largest complaint is its mobile app, and AG has no app at all. Do calls and texts need a phone app in release one, or does the browser carry it until release two?

To confirm with the team

Everything marked in the pieces above, in one place.

Product

  • The feature name Client Conversations and where it sits (open any client → Case → Client Conversations): my proposal for release one.
  • Do client replies come back to the case? That needs inbox sync; for Gmail, Google's yearly security assessment (several weeks).
  • Direct email send from GrantAI: announced on the February 13, 2026 webinar ("you can send emails from here now"), not visible in later demos or the help center. If it shipped, the "Open in mail" hook changes.
  • Request Case Design: the agent picks which emails and call notes go with the request.
  • Reminders: tasks with a due date and a repeat appear in a partner's May 2025 video and nowhere in the 2026 materials. Do they still exist, and can GrantAI's follow-ups become them?
  • Team Activity shows which designed cases got a follow-up (conversation data inside Team Activity).
  • Agency-level switch: the IMO decides whether calling and texting are on for its account, and who pays.

Setup and money

  • Bringing an existing office line over: porting time to confirm; the email promises no date.
  • Calling: AG has no telephony today, and the in-app voice recorder of 2020–2023 is gone. Calling at launch is the heaviest build in this kit.
  • Texting from a number per agent: today every text goes out from one shared number, (225) 535-4832. A number per agent means an SMS provider and A2P 10DLC registration for each. Who files it, AG or the agent? Twilio's figure is up to 5 business days per campaign.
  • Pricing: email and meetings in every plan; calls and texts with N minutes and N texts included, then per minute and per text. My proposal, not AG's price list; which plans get it is question 1.
  • The webinar offer for a case built live, and the attendee offer at 0:22.

Promises AG would be making: questions for AG's compliance owner and founder

  • Data use: can AG tell agents that their clients and conversations stay out of marketing and lead generation, are not sold, and are not shared with carriers or other agencies?
  • Texting: how does the product record a client's yes, how do existing clients give it, and what happens when a client opts out?
  • Recording calls: off by default for the agent, or set by the agency? One rule, printed the same in every piece.
  • Texting number: is it registered in each agent's name, and does AG file it?
  • Retention: how long is each message kept, and can it be exported for audits?
  • Leaving: what goes with an agent who leaves, and who owns the book? About 47 of the 1,927 complaints in the study are about getting your own data out.
  • The agents' launch email can go out co-signed by the IMO, or be handed to the IMO to send.
  • Support: does a person answer 949-600-7707? Support is the most frequent complaint about the tool in the brief.
